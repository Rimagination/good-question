# Contribution And Expression

Use when a reader challenges significance, novelty or value, or before ranking a proposal. Diagnose the question rather than automatically defending the current draft.

## Separate The Two Diagnoses

There may be a presentation problem, an insufficient substantive contribution, or both; check them separately. In Chinese: **可能存在表达问题，也可能是贡献本身不足，目前需要分别检查。** This is a diagnostic principle, not a sentence to insert mechanically into every response.

- **Expression:** can the intended reader identify the question, claimed increment, support and consequence? Locate actual wording or organization defects. Clearer writing can improve communication without establishing significance.
- **Contribution:** does the claimed increment survive comparison with the closest existing work, and why would the remaining difference matter? The proposal's own statement of significance is not independent support.
- State which diagnosis the evidence supports: expression, contribution, both, or unresolved. Do not assume a skeptical reader misunderstood, or reject a result merely because its presentation is weak.
- Repair wording when that is the supported defect. When the issue is substantive, name the missing argument or closest-work comparison. When evidence is insufficient, preserve the candidate and identify a bounded check that could change the judgment.
- Respect language-only requests: preserve meaning and evidence strength without silently redesigning the research or launching retrieval. Keep diagnostic history in review feedback; reader-facing prose should carry the actual argument and material limitations.

## Four Contribution Questions

1. **Does it reveal a previously unclear structural relation?** Identify the relation, mechanism or boundary to be learned, what was unclear, and the evidence that could distinguish it from a familiar effect.
2. **Does it provide a judgment guarantee that existing methods cannot?** Specify the decision or claim being guaranteed, the assumptions and failure conditions, and what the closest method already guarantees.
3. **Does it make a previously difficult computational problem tractable?** Name the obstacle, relevant scale or accuracy requirement, and new capability. Support the comparison with appropriate reasoning, complexity analysis or measurements rather than convenience alone.
4. **Does it correct a systematic error that actually affects choices?** Identify the recurring error, the evidence that it affects decisions, and how correction could change them. An isolated constructed reversal does not establish a systematic practical problem.

These are diagnostic lenses, not four mandatory contributions, an exhaustive taxonomy or an additive score. Other contribution types need their own substantive rationale. For each claimed increment, name the closest alternative, the remaining difference, why it matters, and a feasible discriminating check. In a proposal, distinguish a contribution hypothesis from a demonstrated result; use literature or reasoning checks when experiments are not authorized.

A precise definition, new notation, a falsifiable question, an application list or an acknowledged limitation does not by itself establish significance. Negative results need an informative scope or consequence; their value is not automatic.

Do not postpone the entire value assessment until experiments finish: establish a credible theoretical or practical reason to investigate now, without requiring the final result in advance. Missing evidence leaves the contribution unresolved; it does not by itself justify endorsement or rejection.
